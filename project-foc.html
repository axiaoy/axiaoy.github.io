FOC Current Sense Board

Cornell Electric Vehicles · 2026

  • Altium
  • FOC
  • Current sensing
  • Power electronics

A three-phase current-sensing board that moves the drivetrain to isolated, low-latency phase-current sensing for the transition to field-oriented control.

Motivation

I designed the current-sensing board that enables our team's transition from trapezoidal commutation to field-oriented control (FOC), which reduces torque ripple and improves motor efficiency. FOC runs Clarke and Park transforms on all three phase currents every PWM cycle to regulate torque and flux independently, so its performance depends on how cleanly those currents are measured.

Sensing approach

I evaluated and rejected sensorless back-EMF estimation, which fails at standstill, and shunt resistors, which add power loss and pick up switching noise. Instead, I used isolated closed-loop current transducers, one LEM LZSR 200-P/SP1 per phase, with the phase conductor routed straight through each. This isolates the measurement from the 48 V power stage, adds no loss to the drive path, and keeps enough bandwidth that sensing adds negligible lag to the current loop.

Each transducer output is biased around a 2.5 V mid-rail, so both directions of phase current are captured, and its overcurrent-detect (OCD) output is brought out but left unpopulated on this revision.

Shared reference

The 2.5 V bias comes from an Analog Devices ADR3425 precision reference, wired with its force and sense outputs so the voltage stays accurate at the point of use. One reference feeds the reference input of all three channels, so their offsets track together and don't skew the Clarke and Park transforms.

Board

The sensing front end runs from a 5 V supply. 48 V and ground come in on the power connector, and the three phase-current outputs go out to the motor controller on a separate connector.
