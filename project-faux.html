Front Auxiliary I/O Board

Cornell Electric Vehicles · 2026

  • Altium
  • CAN bus
  • Synchronous buck
  • Board bringup

A distributed front I/O board that moves lighting, horn, steering, and wheel-speed electronics onto the CAN bus, with a custom 48 V to 12 V synchronous buck to drive the horn.

Architecture

I designed, laid out, and brought up our vehicle's front auxiliary I/O board, replacing the single central auxiliary board used in previous years with a distributed architecture. Placing the electronics beside the loads they control cut the cross-vehicle harness down to power and the CAN bus. The board is built around a Raspberry Pi Pico with a CAN controller on its SPI bus; it receives lighting and horn commands over CAN and publishes steering angle and wheel speed back onto the bus.

Load switching

Each 12 V load is switched low-side from a Pico GPIO. The headlights and indicators use NVR5198NLT3G MOSFETs and the horn uses a larger IPD50N06S2L-13. Every gate is driven through a 100 Ω series resistor with a 10 kΩ pulldown, and a B560C Schottky diode clamps the horn's inductive kickback.

Power

The horn's current draw exceeded what integrated regulators can practically deliver from a 48 V input, so I designed a 48 V to 12 V synchronous buck around a controller with external MOSFETs, deriving the switching frequency, inductor, and capacitors from the controller's design equations. I sized it with current and voltage margin and followed it with LDOs for the 5 V and 3.3 V rails.

Sensing

Robust sensors were selected, including a non-contact Hall-effect absolute encoder (Piher PST-360G2), which keeps the true steering angle through power loss without homing, and inductive proximity sensors (Omron E2B-S08KN04-MC-B1) measure wheel RPM. The two RPM signals are divided from 12 V down to the Pico's logic level, and the steering output is divided into one of the Pico's ADC inputs.
